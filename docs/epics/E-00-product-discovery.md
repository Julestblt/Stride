# E-00: Product discovery and first-release definition

GitHub execution issue: [#1 Validate the initial athlete segment and first-release boundary](https://github.com/Julestblt/Stride/issues/1)

## Objective

Validate who Stride should serve first, which running-coaching problem matters most, and what outcome is small enough to guide a first release.

## Scope

- Validate the initial athlete segment and event context
- Gather evidence about the athlete's current training workflow and unmet need
- Decide whether Garmin-first data is necessary to deliver first value
- Refine the candidate first-release journey, goals, non-goals, and success measures
- Update the product brief and PRD with evidence, decisions, and remaining assumptions

## Dependencies

The current product vision, principles, and draft product artifacts are available as starting context. No application implementation is required.

## Expected user value

The product team and future implementation agents can make scope decisions from athlete evidence instead of treating the current product hypotheses as validated facts.

## Major risks

- Planning documents are mistaken for evidence of athlete demand
- Garmin integration dominates the first release before its value is validated
- A wide product vision becomes an unfocused first release

## Completion criteria

- A primary athlete segment and one high-value journey are supported by direct evidence or an explicit research limitation.
- The first-release boundary and non-goals are agreed and reflected in the brief and PRD.
- Data permissions, Garmin dependency, and coaching safety questions have owners or documented next decisions.
- Success measures and a pilot approach are defined or explicitly deferred with a reason.
