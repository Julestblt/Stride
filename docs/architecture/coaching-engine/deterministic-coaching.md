# Deterministic coaching and validation

- Status: Accepted (RFC #86, amended by decisions D6 and D8 in #94)
- Decision record: [ADR 0012](../../adr/0012-evidence-graded-guardrails-and-health-boundary.md)

## Principles

1. Code owns numbers. Volumes, dates, paces, heart-rate bounds, loads and thresholds are computed, never generated.
2. Every rule declares an evidence grade:
   - **evidence**: a primary study supports the direction and the threshold for this population;
   - **convention**: a reasonable default without direct evidence for the threshold;
   - **integrity**: data and time correctness.
3. A missing signal is never favorable. When a rule cannot be evaluated, it reports `skipped` with a reason, and the uncertainty of the proposal rises.
4. Estimates are labeled as estimates with their confidence. Garmin composite scores (training readiness, Body Battery, training status) are presented as Garmin data, not reinterpreted as physiological measurements.
5. Passing the validator means a proposal respects Stride's rules and the athlete's declared constraints. It does not mean the plan is medically safe, and no copy says so.
6. No human expert reviews plans (D8); the athlete reviews every plan and stays in control of it. Defaults come from the reference project ai-running-coach or are labeled Stride conventions. The validator blocks only integrity, the athlete's declared constraints and health stops; everything else is a warning shown to the athlete, so the Coach keeps room for judgment.

## Evidence notes

- **Single-session spikes.** In 5,205 adult runners followed for 18 months with Garmin data, a session exceeding the longest run of the previous 30 days by more than 10–30% was associated with a higher overuse injury rate (hazard rate ratio 1.64), more than 30–100% with 1.52, and more than 100% with 2.28. The acute:chronic ratio showed a negative dose-response and the week-to-week ratio no relationship ([Frandsen et al., Br J Sports Med 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-109380)). This is observational evidence of association, not proof that capping spikes prevents injury, but it is the best-matched evidence for Stride's population and data source.
- **Weekly progression.** A randomized trial of a graded 10%-rule programme in 532 novice runners found no reduction in injuries (20.8% versus 20.3%) ([Buist et al., Am J Sports Med 2008](https://doi.org/10.1177/0363546507307505)). In 874 novice runners, weekly progression above 30% over two weeks was associated with more distance-related injuries, without statistical significance overall ([Nielsen et al., JOSPT 2014](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2014.5164)). Weekly caps are therefore warnings and labeled conventions.
- **Acute:chronic ratio.** "There is no evidence supporting the use of ACWR in training-load-management systems or for training recommendations aimed at reducing injury risk" ([Impellizzeri et al., IJSPP 2020](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2019-0864)). Stride shows Garmin's load for context and never blocks on the ratio.
- **Taper.** A meta-analysis of 27 studies found a 2-week taper with volume exponentially reduced by 41–60% and intensity and frequency maintained to be the most efficient strategy in competitive athletes ([Bosquet et al., Med Sci Sports Exerc 2007](https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e31806010e0)).
- **Intensity distribution.** Polarized training improved VO₂ peak more than other distributions mainly in short interventions and highly trained athletes, with similar effects on time-trial and threshold outcomes ([Oliveira et al., Sports Med 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02034-z)). Template splits (around 80% easy) are conventions.
- **HRV-guided training.** HRV-guided training improved vagal HRV indices but showed only small, non-significant benefits for aerobic fitness and performance ([Manresa-Rocamora et al., IJERPH 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph181910299)). HRV modulates intensity choices; it never gates training on its own.
- **Risk factors.** Previous injury in the last 12 months is the most consistently reported risk factor ([Saragiotto et al., Sports Med 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-014-0194-6)); the broader evidence is multifactorial and mostly low quality ([umbrella review, J Sport Health Sci 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2024.04.011)). Injury history raises caution and lowers ramp rates.
- **Health boundary.** Clinical guidance for respiratory illness and for energy deficiency is written for clinicians ([IOC consensus 2022](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2022-105567), [IOC REDs consensus 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994)); Stride refers rather than replicates it, and excludes race-weight advice.

## Plan construction defaults (conventions)

| Topic                | Default                                                                                                           | Source of the choice                                      |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Peak volume          | Average of the last four complete weeks × template factor (100 / first-week share of peak, about 1.09–1.21)       | Reference project rule; keeps progression within warnings |
| Lead-in ramp         | +4% per week, capped by P1 and availability, recovery week every fourth week                                      | Convention                                                |
| Recovery week        | Every 4 weeks at 80% of the last full week by default; the Coach adapts the cadence to the athlete's own recovery | Reference project default; recovery is individual         |
| Long-run progression | Respects E1/E2 against the rolling 30-day maximum                                                                 | Evidence-led                                              |
| Detail horizon       | Every week fully detailed at creation; the weekly review re-checks the coming week and proposes changes           | Product decision D6                                       |
| Intensity targets    | Heart rate from Stride zones when calibrated; pace from recent comparable efforts; otherwise RPE and talk test    | Data availability                                         |
| Event families       | Road 5–10 km, half, marathon; trail of every distance, with a low-confidence label above 60 km; base building     | Product decision D8                                       |

## Validation pipeline

1. Parse and type-check operations (Zod).
2. Resolve against the base version; reject frozen or unknown targets (I1–I4).
3. Build the candidate week or weeks, then evaluate C, H, E, P and D rules over the candidate and its neighbours.
4. Produce a report: rule id, severity, observed value, threshold, sessions involved, grade, skip reason.
5. If any `block`, return the report to the model as structured feedback; the model may revise twice. After that, the workflow falls back to the most conservative valid alternative it can build deterministically (for example "keep the plan, reduce intensity") or returns no proposal with an explanation.
6. Store the final report on the proposal; re-run steps 2–4 at apply time with the current context.

## Missing and uncertain data

| Situation                                                                  | Behavior                                                                                                                                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Incomplete Garmin import                                                   | Compute only over synchronized weeks; state the window; if activities are still importing and less than 8 weeks are available, ask whether to wait or plan now with low confidence |
| Missing physiological data (no calibrated heart-rate zones, no resting HR) | Use RPE and pace targets; omit heart-rate targets; record a calibration gap and, when useful, suggest a controlled effort to calibrate                                             |
| Insufficient training history (under 4 weeks or under 1 hour per week)     | Base-building template only; ask for current weekly time and longest run; feasibility confidence low                                                                               |
| Stale recovery data (no observation for the last 2 days)                   | Readiness `unknown`; never treat as green; rely on check-in; no load increase justified by readiness                                                                               |
| Athlete-reported pain or illness                                           | H1/H2 apply; referral copy; return-to-running proposals only after the athlete marks the condition resolved, starting below the previous load                                      |
| Unrealistic objective                                                      | Feasibility verdict with the facts that drive it and alternatives; never silently produce an impossible plan                                                                       |
| Uncertain performance estimate                                             | Ranges with confidence, labeled "estimation Stride"; never shown as a measured result                                                                                              |
| Conflicting data (athlete says a run happened, Garmin has nothing)         | Athlete declaration wins for adherence with source `athlete`; load computations use only measured activities and say so                                                            |
