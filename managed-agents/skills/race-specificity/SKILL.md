# Race and terrain specificity

- 10 km: VO2max intervals (3-5 min at 5 km pace) and threshold work; specific blocks at 10 km pace in the last four weeks.
- Half marathon: threshold blocks (2-3 x 10-15 min), half-pace segments inside long runs in the specific phase.
- Marathon: long runs up to 2 h 45-3 h, marathon-pace segments growing to 12-16 km inside long runs; fueling practised on every long run over 90 minutes.
- Trail: weekly elevation grows by at most 15% per week; hill repeats, uphill hiking with poles for steep races, technical descents practised; prescribe long runs in time and elevation, not pace.
- Ultra (above 42.2 km): back-to-back long runs on the weekend in the specific phase, night or race-terrain rehearsal, fueling on every long run. After an ultra, three days without running.
- Ultra of 60 km or more: build time on feet, prescribed in time and elevation. Peak weeks of about 8-12 hours for 80-130 km (more for 100 miles) when the proven volume and the weeks available allow it; walking and hiking count. Long runs grow by about 30 minutes every one or two long runs, up to 4-6 hours. Back-to-back weekends every other week in the specific phase (Saturday 3-5 hours, Sunday 1.5-3 hours). One specific long outing of 5-7 hours on race-like terrain with full nutrition rehearsal 5-7 weeks before the race, ideally a B race. The longest weekend block reaches about 40% of the expected race duration. Above 60 km the plan carries a low-confidence note.
- Recurring pain (for example shins) during an ultra build: keep the generic load rules, favour hiking, soft terrain and cross-training for extra time on feet, and let Medical set the limits.
- Preparation races: a B or C race two to four weeks before the A race is a hard workout with a two- or three-day mini-taper and two easy days after; it never replaces the A-race taper.
- Race after the current objective: plan recovery first (at least one week after a marathon, two after an ultra), then a short rebuild, a specific block and a taper sized to the new race. Ten weeks or less between two A races leaves room for one specific block, not a full cycle.
- Target realism: compare the target with recent results (Riegel-style equivalence). If the target needs more than about 5% improvement per three months of training, or the history is shorter than eight weeks, do not plan for it: plan for a realistic target, state it, and explain why.
